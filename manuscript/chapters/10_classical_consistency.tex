\chapter{Classical and Astrophysical Consistency}
\label{ch:ch10_classical}

The question of this chapter is what the classical readings of the arena say about observed physics. It reads the rest energy
of a field point in the sky, the pointwise rest energy of isotropic radiation, pair creation, several particles in three times, the rate
$\kappa=c/\ell$ and the sky, and time-odd invariants, clocks and the constancy of masses. Each comparison is kept within its scope: energy
densities are compared in one environment, and the traceless stress tensor of radiation, not its density, decides whether it can stand in for
matter; the distribution law of the rest energy holds in the classical Gaussian model; vacuum, two-photon and one-photon pair creation are kept
apart, and the pair invariant is an identity of classical densities, not a threshold or a rate; the nuclear figure is a conditional sensitivity
estimate; the birefringence bound on $\kappa$ holds within a stated coupling model; the couplings of masses to clocks and to gravity are stated,
and the clock tests constrain differential effects only; and the signed clock energy is not a mass. No observation singles out the arena; the
bounds constrain readings, not the algebra.

\section{Classical readings: astronomy and cosmology}
\label{e7:sec:main}

This chapter reads the results of the preceding chapters in the sense of classical physics, astronomy and cosmology. Five
classes are kept apart. A statement is \emph{inherited} when it is ordinary physics, true in any notation, and cited from the literature. It is
a \emph{reading} (interpretive, in the classes of this manuscript) when it identifies a quantity of the arena with an observed one; we say what the
identification needs. It is a \emph{constraint} when, if a reading is adopted, an observation bounds a parameter. The constraint is then fixed
arithmetic on cited inputs, conditional on the reading. It is \emph{obstructed} when observation or inherited physics excludes the reading, and
\emph{open} otherwise. Mathematical statements carry the classes fixed and restated as before.

One fact governs the whole section. The arena is scale-free: the rate $\kappa=c/\ell$ of the interior flow has no fixed length $\ell$, the
identification $mc^2=\hbar\kappa$ is added, and the multiscale ratio of \cref{cor:multiscale} is an input (\cref{cl:noscale},
\cref{cor:multiscale} and~\cref{sec:resttworot}). No number of astronomy or cosmology follows from the arena alone. Every number below is either
inherited arithmetic on observed inputs or a bound on a parameter of a reading.

The answers, with their classes, are these. (1) The rest energy $u_0$ of a field point is the energy density in the frames where $\bE\parallel\Btext$
(inherited). Its stress is a tension $u_0$ along the common axis and a pressure $u_0$ across it, and every isotropic ensemble of field points
gravitates as radiation, $w=\tfrac13$, whatever its $u_0$. So the reading ``field rest energy is dark matter'' is obstructed
(\cref{e7:sec:point}). (2) In the classical Gaussian model of isotropic thermal radiation the ratio $E_0/E_6$ at a point has density $2t$ on
$[0,1]$, independent of the energy, so $\langle E_0\rangle/\langle E_6\rangle=\tfrac23$ (fixed within the model). Every plane wave is null, but the
random superposition has $q\neq0$ almost everywhere. ``The CMB is locally massive'' is a reading; for the quantised field it needs a smearing,
an operator ordering and a measurement model, and it says nothing about a photon mass (\cref{e7:sec:cmb}). (3) The pair invariant of two null fields (\cref{d6:thm:pair}) vanishes on the same configurations as the Breit--Wheeler invariant. The thresholds of the gamma-ray horizon need $m_e$,
which the arena does not supply. The vacuum pair rate depends on the canonical electric field $\epsilon_{\rm can}$, not on $E_0=\half(\epsilon_{\rm can}^2+\beta_{\rm can}^2)$,
so magnetar fields above $B_Q$ create no pairs from the vacuum; the pairs of magnetised environments are made by photons converting in the
field, and the photons supply the energy (inherited; \cref{e7:sec:pairs}). (4) Two six-momenta at mutual rest with temporal angle $\vartheta$
have $M^2=m_1^2+m_2^2+2m_1m_2\cos\vartheta$. In three times ``uphill'' decays are kinematically open, and one clock closes them (inherited, with
an exact example); without amplitudes this motivates the reduction and bounds no lifetime. Under the reading, and under assumptions about the reaction that the arena does not derive, the $E=mc^2$ test of neutron
capture on $^{28}$Si corresponds to a temporal angle of order $10^{-4}$~rad between the neutron and the nucleus; this is a conditional
sensitivity estimate, not a measured angle (\cref{e7:sec:sixmomenta}). (5) A duality rotation of a null field is a rotation about its
propagation axis (fixed). If the interior flow acted on free light as a duality rotation at a constant rate in cosmic time, a coupling the arena
does not supply, CMB polarisation would bound that rate by $2.5\times10^{-20}\,{\rm s^{-1}}$, $10^{40.5}$ below $m_ec^2/\hbar$, on the unwrapped
small-angle branch of that propagation hypothesis; the aliases $\beta+n\pi$ are not excluded by this division, and the bound concerns the model,
not the arena (\cref{e7:sec:rate}). (6) The three-manifold time-odd invariant (\cref{d7:thm:three}) parallels the Jarlskog invariant only as a reading. A temporal $b$-field acting on the clocks of matter is
bounded by the constancy of $m_p/m_e$ and by the equivalence principle, as conditional constraints on differential effects, under stated
couplings of the masses to clocks and to gravity (\cref{e7:sec:timeodd}). \Cref{e7:sec:summary} collects the claims (\cref{e7:tab:claims}).
Identifiers e7-$n$ refer to the \texttt{PASS} lines of \texttt{e7\_classical.py}, and z5-$n$ to those of the adjudication checks
\texttt{z5\_classical\_adjudication.py}; physical constants are CODATA values from \texttt{scipy.constants}.

\paragraph{Notation.} SI units. The frame variable is $\bB=-\Btext$, with $\Btext$ the textbook field (\cref{sec:conventions}), $z_c=c\bB+i\bE$,
$q_c=z_c\cdot z_c$, $u=\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}2|z_c|^2$, $u_0=\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}2|q_c|$, $\mathbf g=\bE\times\Btext/(\mu_0c^2)$ and $\rho_0=u_0/c^2$. Then
$u^2=c^2|\mathbf g|^2+u_0^2$ (\cref{en:SI}; restated, e7-2). In Heaviside--Lorentz units with $c=1$, $z=\bB+i\bE$, $E_6=\half|z|^2$,
$E_0=\half|q|$, $q=z\cdot z=|\Btext|^2-|\bE|^2-2i\,\bE\cdot\Btext$. The realisation is that of \cref{app:realisation}, and $G$ acts on $z$ as $i$ (e7-1).

\subsection{The rest energy of a field point in the sky}
\label{e7:sec:point}

The infimum of $u$ over frames is $u_0$. For a non-null field ($q\neq0$) it is an attained minimum, exactly in the frames where the Poynting
vector vanishes, and there $\bE\parallel\Btext$ or one of them vanishes (\cref{en:min}). For a nonzero null field $u_0=0$ is approached but not
attained: a boost along a plane wave gives $u(v)=u(0)(1-v)/(1+v)>0$ for $|v|<1$. This is inherited: the classification of a field at a point
\cite{Synge56} and the problem of the frame with parallel fields in \S25 of \cite{LandauLifshitz2}. Let
$\Theta^{00}=u$, $\Theta^{0i}=(\bE\times\Btext)_i$, $\Theta^{ij}=-(E_iE_j+B_{{\rm t}i}B_{{\rm t}j})+u\,\delta_{ij}$ be the stress--energy tensor of the field
(Heaviside--Lorentz, $c=1$).

\begin{proposition}[The stress of a field point]
\label{e7:prop:stress}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $\Theta^\mu{}_\mu=0$ and $\Theta^\mu{}_\lambda \Theta^\lambda{}_\nu=E_0^2\,\delta^\mu{}_\nu$.
\item If $\bE=\epsilon_{\rm can}\,\mathbf n$ and $\Btext=\beta_{\rm can}\,\mathbf n$ with $|\mathbf n|=1$, then $\Theta^{00}=u_0$, $\Theta^{0i}=0$ and $\Theta^{ij}=u_0(\delta_{ij}-2n_in_j)$:
a tension $u_0$ along $\mathbf n$ and a pressure $u_0$ in the two directions across it.
\item In every ensemble of field points whose mean stress is isotropic in some frame, $\langle \Theta^{ij}\rangle=p\,\delta^{ij}$ with
$p=\tfrac13\langle \Theta^{00}\rangle$, whatever the distribution of $u_0$. For field points at rest with isotropically distributed axes,
$p=\tfrac13\langle u_0\rangle$.
\item In a Friedmann--Lema\^itre universe a component with $p=w\rho c^2$ obeys $\dot\rho=-3(1+w)\rho\,\dot a/a$. So $\rho\propto a^{-4}$ for every
such ensemble of free fields, against $a^{-3}$ for dust.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\sum_i\Theta^{ii}=-(|\bE|^2+|\Btext|^2)+3u=u$, so $\Theta^\mu{}_\mu=\Theta^{00}-\sum_i\Theta^{ii}=0$; the square is the Rainich identity (\cref{en:rainich}). (ii) Substitute; $u=\half(\epsilon_{\rm can}^2+\beta_{\rm can}^2)=u_0$ since $|q|=\epsilon_{\rm can}^2+\beta_{\rm can}^2$ when $\bE\parallel\Btext$, and $\bE\times\Btext=0$.
(iii) Tracelessness gives $\langle \Theta^{00}\rangle=\sum_i\langle \Theta^{ii}\rangle=3p$. In the second case $\langle n_in_j\rangle=\tfrac13\delta_{ij}$, so
$\langle \Theta^{ij}\rangle=u_0(1-\tfrac23)\delta_{ij}$. (iv) Integrate the continuity equation.
\end{proof}

Inherited (\S\S33 and~35 of \cite{LandauLifshitz2}; \cite{Rainich1925}, as restated in \cref{en:SI}; \cite{WeinbergCosmo08}), checked exactly (e7-3, e7-4). A
bounded static configuration together with its sources has total mass $U/c^2$, with the field stresses balanced by those of the sources (von Laue's
theorem \cite{Laue1911}; \cref{en:whole}). Field energy then gravitates as part of the mass of a matter system, and the matter fixes
its share.

\begin{corollary}[Field rest energy is not dark matter]
\label{e7:cor:dm}
Reading $\rho_0=u_0/c^2$ as a rest-mass density does not make field rest energy behave as matter in cosmology. The reading ``field rest energy is
dark matter'' is obstructed by inherited physics.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
What gravitates is $\Theta^{\mu\nu}$, and the reading leaves it unchanged. The dark matter of the standard model of cosmology is pressureless
since before recombination, with $\Omega_ch^2=0.120\pm0.001$ \cite{Planck18VI}. By \cref{e7:prop:stress}(iii)--(iv) every isotropic ensemble
of free field points has $w=\tfrac13$ and redshifts as $a^{-4}$, whatever $u_0$. A field point with $u_0>0$ is at rest only in the sense of
\cref{e7:prop:stress}(ii): its stress is not that of dust, and a free field propagates at $c$. The only configurations whose field energy
weighs like rest mass are bound ones, by von Laue's theorem, and there the mass belongs to the matter system. The amounts are also small,
when local densities are compared with local ones and cosmological means with cosmological means. Near the Sun the galactic magnetic energy
density, $0.22$ to $0.89\,{\rm eV\,cm^{-3}}$ for $3$ to $6\,\mu$G, is between $4\times10^{-10}$ and $4.5\times10^{-9}$ of the local dark-matter
density, $0.2$ to $0.56\,{\rm GeV\,cm^{-3}}$ in the measurements collated by Read \cite{Read14} (z5-1). In the cosmological mean, the rest energy of
the CMB (\cref{e7:sec:cmb}), $0.17\,{\rm eV\,cm^{-3}}$, is $1.4\times10^{-4}$ of the mean cold-dark-matter energy density $1.26\,{\rm keV\,cm^{-3}}$
(e7-26, z5-1); the cosmological mean of the magnetic energy density is not known, and no comparison is made for it. The cause of the
obstruction is the tracelessness, not the deficit.
\end{proof}

The fields of astronomy are, to a good approximation, magnetic in the frame of the conducting matter, where $\bE\approx0$, so $u=u_0=B^2/(2\mu_0)$
and $\rho_0=B^2/(2\mu_0c^2)$ there. \Cref{e7:tab:fields} gives the arithmetic (e7-5) for representative inputs: the geomagnetic field at the surface
(about $50\,\mu$T; total intensity maps of \cite{Alken21}), a sunspot umbra ($0.3$~T \cite{Solanki03}), a strongly magnetic white dwarf (isolated
magnetic white dwarfs reach about $10^5$~T \cite{Ferrario15}), the surface of a radio pulsar (about $10^8$~T \cite{LorimerKramer05}), a magnetar
($10^{10}$--$10^{11}$~T \cite{KaspiBeloborodov17}) and the galactic field (a few microgauss, about $6\,\mu$G in total near the Sun
\cite{Beck16}).

\subsection{The pointwise rest energy of isotropic radiation}
\label{e7:sec:cmb}

\begin{proposition}[Rest-energy fraction of a Gaussian field]
\label{e7:prop:gauss}
Let $z=\mathbf x+i\mathbf y$ with $\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in\R^3$ independent and $N(0,\sigma^2\id)$, and let $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2$ be the
eigenvalues of the Gram matrix $\mathcal G_{xy}$ of $(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$. Then $|q|=\lambda_1-\lambda_2$ and $|z|^2=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$. The ratio
$t=E_0/E_6=|z\cdot z|/|z|^2\in[0,1]$ has density $2t$ (distribution function $t^2$), is independent of $|z|^2$, and has mean $\tfrac23$ and
second moment $\half$. Hence $\langle E_0\rangle/\langle E_6\rangle=\tfrac23$, and $q\neq0$ with probability one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$|q|^2=(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf x-\mathbf y\cdot\mathbf y)^2+4(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf y)^2=(\tr\mathcal G_{xy})^2-4\det\mathcal G_{xy}=(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)^2$. $\mathcal G_{xy}$ is a real Wishart
matrix $W_2(3,\sigma^2\id)$, whose eigenvalues have the density $\propto(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)e^{-(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)/2\sigma^2}$ on
$\lambda_1>\lambda_2>0$; the general factor $\prod_i\lambda_i^{(n-p-1)/2}$ is $1$ at $n=3$, $p=2$ \cite{Muirhead82}. With $s=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$,
$t=(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)/s$ and Jacobian $s/2$ the density becomes $\propto s^2e^{-s/2\sigma^2}\cdot t$ on $s>0$, $0<t<1$. It factorises, the first
factor being the $\chi^2_6$ law of $|z|^2/\sigma^2$. So $t$ has density $2t$ and is independent of $s$, and
$\langle ts\rangle/\langle s\rangle=\langle t\rangle=\tfrac23$.
\end{proof}

The law is inherited: $f(t)=2t$ is the eigenvalue law of a real Wishart matrix with $N=2$, $K=3$ \cite[eq.~(4.5)]{ZyczkowskiSommers01}; what the arena adds
is the electromagnetic reading of $t$ as $E_0/E_6$. Checked symbolically (e7-7); by Isserlis' theorem, $\langle|q|^2\rangle=24\sigma^4$, $\langle|z|^4\rangle=48\sigma^4$,
$\langle|q|^4\rangle=1920\sigma^8$ and $\langle|z|^8\rangle=5760\sigma^8$, whose ratios are the moments $\half$ and $\tfrac13$ of the density $2t$
(e7-8); and by Monte Carlo with $2\times10^6$ samples, with mean $0.6665$, Kolmogorov distance $7.5\times10^{-4}$ to $t^2$ and correlation
$-1.1\times10^{-3}$ between $t$ and $|z|^2$ (e7-9).

\emph{The model.} Thermal light is a circular complex Gaussian field, by the central limit theorem over many independent emitters
(Sections~4.2 and~4.3 of \cite{Goodman15}); this is inherited. For isotropic, unpolarised blackbody radiation at a point, isotropy gives
$\langle E_iE_j\rangle=\langle B_{{\rm t}i}B_{{\rm t}j}\rangle=\tfrac13u\,\delta_{ij}$ and $\langle E_iB_{{\rm t}j}\rangle=\tfrac13\langle\bE\cdot\Btext\rangle\delta_{ij}$,
and parity invariance gives $\langle\bE\cdot\Btext\rangle=0$. The six components of $z$ are therefore independent with a common variance, which
is the hypothesis of \cref{e7:prop:gauss}, and $\langle E_6\rangle=u$, $\langle E_0\rangle=\tfrac23u$. The distribution $2t$ is a statement of this
classical Gaussian model, and only of it; it is not a prediction of the arena.

\emph{Scope of the model}. For the quantised radiation field the statement needs a coarse-graining and a measurement model, and in
general an operator ordering. A quantum field has no value at a point, so the fields must be smeared. If all six components are smeared with
one spherically symmetric test function $f$ at one time, they commute, since $[E_i(f),B_{{\rm t}j}(f)]$ is proportional to
$\epsilon_{ijk}\int f\,\partial_kf\,d^3x=0$ (z5-2). A joint measurement of them then has a distribution. In a thermal state, which is Gaussian
\cite{Glauber63}, that distribution is Gaussian, and by isotropy, parity and duality invariance its six components are independent with a
common variance. The law $2t$ then holds for the smeared ratio. Since it does not depend on the variance, it holds in the vacuum as well: it
describes the shape of a Gaussian ensemble, not a property of the CMB. With different test functions, or at different times, the components need
not commute (z5-2; concentric radial test functions can commute), $|q|$ is not a polynomial in them, and an ordering or a quasi-probability must be
chosen, which is then not a distribution of measured ratios. The smeared distribution includes the vacuum fluctuations and is not by itself a
statement about a renormalised local rest-energy observable. Nor is the law a statement about photons: every mode is null, and nothing here gives the photon a rest mass.

\emph{The reading.} Every plane wave is null, $q=0$ (\cref{thm:axis}). A random superposition has $q\neq0$ almost everywhere, with
mean rest-energy density $\tfrac23u$ in the classical model. To say that the CMB is ``locally massive'' is a reading. Its content is
$\langle E_0\rangle=\tfrac23u$, under the assumptions just stated, and it says nothing about gravitation, which sees the traceless $\Theta^{\mu\nu}$ of \cref{e7:prop:stress}. The whole behaves differently. Radiation in a box at
rest in the CMB frame has $\mathbf P=0$ and $Mc^2=U$, while $\int E_0=\tfrac23U$ on average; this is the strict inequality $M\ge\int E_0$ of
\cref{en:whole}(2), strict because the local energy--momenta are not proportional. Numbers (e7-10): today
$u=aT_{\rm CMB}^4=4.17\times10^{-14}\,{\rm J\,m^{-3}}$, $\langle u_0\rangle=2.78\times10^{-14}\,{\rm J\,m^{-3}}$ and
$\langle\rho_0\rangle=3.10\times10^{-31}\,{\rm kg\,m^{-3}}=3.6\times10^{-5}\rho_{\rm crit}$, with $\rho_{\rm crit}=3H_0^2/8\pi G_{\rm N}$ for
$H_0=67.4\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$ \cite{Planck18VI}. Like the whole CMB ($\Omega_\gamma=5.4\times10^{-5}$), this density redshifts as $a^{-4}$.

\subsection{Two from one in the sky: pair creation}
\label{e7:sec:pairs}

\emph{Two photons.} For null four-momenta $k_1,k_2$ with energies $E_1,E_2$ at angle $\vartheta$, $s=(k_1+k_2)^2=2E_1E_2(1-\cos\vartheta)$, and
$\gamma\gamma\to e^+e^-$ needs $s\ge4m_e^2c^4$ \cite{BreitWheeler34}. Head-on, $E_1E_2\ge(m_ec^2)^2$ (e7-11). On CMB photons of mean energy
$\pi^4kT_{\rm CMB}/30\zeta(3)=6.3\times10^{-4}$~eV the threshold is $4.1\times10^{14}$~eV, and on photons of energy $kT_{\rm CMB}$ it is $1.1\times10^{15}$~eV. On
optical and near-infrared extragalactic background light, $0.1$--$3$~eV, it is $0.09$--$2.6$~TeV. These thresholds enter the gamma-ray horizon
\cite{Nikishov62,GouldSchreder67,DwekKrennrich13}, which is an optical-depth condition and needs in addition the background spectrum, the cross
section, the distance and the cosmological evolution; Gould and Schr\'eder predicted the cut-off of the cosmic photon spectrum above $10^{14}$~eV by
the $3$~K photons. The process has been observed with quasi-real, linearly polarised photons of ultraperipheral Au+Au collisions, with angular
distributions in accord with QED \cite{STAR21}. All of this is inherited.

\emph{Relation to the arena.} For two null fields $M_{\rm pair}=|z_1\cdot z_2|=(E_6^{(1)}E_6^{(2)})^{1/2}(1-\cos\vartheta_{12})$, with $\vartheta_{12}$ the
angle between the Poynting vectors (\cref{d6:thm:pair}(v); restated, e7-12). One null field does not become a massive pair under any
covariant duplication, and no closure dynamics fills an empty constituent (\cref{d6:cor:nopair}; \cref{e2:cor:field}). The precise
relation is this. $M_{\rm pair}$ and $s$ vanish on the same configurations, the collinear ones, and are positive on all others: the selection
rule ``one photon makes no pair, two non-collinear photons can'' is shared. The functions differ, and so do the objects: $M_{\rm pair}$ is an
invariant of two classical field densities, an energy density, and $s^{1/2}$ the invariant energy of a two-photon state. $M_{\rm pair}$ is linear
in $1-\cos\vartheta$, because field densities are quadratic in the fields; $s$ is linear in $1-\cos\vartheta$, so the photon pair mass $s^{1/2}$
goes as its square root. The identity is not a threshold, a cross section or a rate.
The threshold $2m_ec^2$ needs $m_e$, which the arena does not supply (\cref{cl:noscale}). The gamma-ray horizon tests QED and the
background light, not the arena.

\emph{Constant fields.} If $q\neq0$ there are frames with $\bE=\epsilon_{\rm can}\,\mathbf n$, $\Btext=\beta_{\rm can}\,\mathbf n$ (\S25 of \cite{LandauLifshitz2}).

\begin{proposition}[Canonical fields]
\label{e7:prop:canon}
In such a frame, with $\epsilon_{\rm can}\ge0$ and $\beta_{\rm can}$ real,
\[
\epsilon_{\rm can}^2=\half(|q|-\Re q),\qquad \beta_{\rm can}^2=\half(|q|+\Re q),\qquad E_0=\half|q|=\half(\epsilon_{\rm can}^2+\beta_{\rm can}^2).
\]
The characteristic polynomial of $F^\mu{}_\nu$ is $\lambda^4+(|\Btext|^2-|\bE|^2)\lambda^2-(\bE\cdot\Btext)^2=(\lambda^2-\epsilon_{\rm can}^2)(\lambda^2+\beta_{\rm can}^2)$, with
eigenvalues $\pm\epsilon_{\rm can}$ and $\pm i\beta_{\rm can}$: $\epsilon_{\rm can}=\epsilon_E$ and $|\beta_{\rm can}|=\epsilon_M$ of \cref{sec:orth:what}. A purely magnetic $F$
generates rotations and a purely electric one boosts.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$q$ is invariant under the Lorentz group, which acts on $z$ through $SO(3,\C)$. In the canonical frame $\bE\cdot\Btext=\epsilon_{\rm can}\beta_{\rm can}$, so
$q=\beta_{\rm can}^2-\epsilon_{\rm can}^2-2i\epsilon_{\rm can}\beta_{\rm can}=(\beta_{\rm can}-i\epsilon_{\rm can})^2$, $|q|=\epsilon_{\rm can}^2+\beta_{\rm can}^2$ and $\Re q=\beta_{\rm can}^2-\epsilon_{\rm can}^2$. The coefficients of the
characteristic polynomial are invariants and take these values. A real antisymmetric $F$ exponentiates to rotations, a symmetric one to boosts.
\end{proof}

Inherited, checked exactly and on $200$ random fields to $10^{-14}$ (e7-13). The vacuum decay rate in a constant field is
\[
w=\frac{e^2\epsilon_{\rm can}\beta_{\rm can}}{4\pi^2\hbar^2c}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1n\coth\frac{n\pi\beta_{\rm can}}{\epsilon_{\rm can}}\,\exp\Bigl(-\frac{n\pi E_S}{\epsilon_{\rm can}}\Bigr),\qquad
E_S=\frac{m_e^2c^3}{e\hbar}=1.32\times10^{18}\,{\rm V\,m^{-1}},
\]
in Heaviside--Lorentz units \cite{Schwinger51,HeisenbergEuler36}. It is exponential in $\epsilon_{\rm can}$ and vanishes for $\epsilon_{\rm can}=0$, whatever $\beta_{\rm can}$;
\Cref{sec:orth:nomass} records the same fact for non-orthogonal fields. At fixed $E_0$ the first term varies by more than a factor $10$
between the electric and the magnetic canonical field and vanishes at the magnetic end; at fixed $\epsilon_{\rm can}$ it grows with $\beta_{\rm can}$ (e7-27). The
critical magnetic field is $B_Q=m_e^2c^2/(e\hbar)=4.41\times10^9$~T. Radio pulsars sit at $0.02\,B_Q$; magnetars, at $10^{10}$--$10^{11}$~T, exceed
it by factors $2$--$23$ \cite{KaspiBeloborodov17} (e7-14). Yet their fields are magnetic, $\epsilon_{\rm can}\approx0$, and create no pairs from the vacuum by
this mechanism, although their $E_0$ is the largest in nature. Hence the reading ``the rest energy of a field point materialises as pairs'' is
obstructed: the pair rate is not a function of $E_0$.

\emph{Pulsars.} In a corotating, force-free magnetosphere $\bE\cdot\Btext=0$ \cite{GoldreichJulian69}. Pair cascades need gaps where
$\bE\cdot\Btext\neq0$, which accelerate the primary particles \cite{RudermanSutherland75}. The curvature gamma-rays of the primaries convert into
pairs in the strong magnetic field \cite{Sturrock71}. That one-photon conversion takes its energy from the photon; the magnetic field supplies
momentum, not rest energy (inherited).

\emph{Three mechanisms kept apart}. (a) Vacuum creation by an electric field, the Schwinger process: non-perturbative, with the
rate above, and absent in a constant, purely magnetic field. (b) Photon--photon creation, the Breit--Wheeler process: a threshold
$s\ge4m_e^2c^4$ and a QED cross section. (c) One-photon creation in a magnetic field, $\gamma\to e^+e^-$, possible only with the field present: the
photon supplies the energy and must have $\hbar\omega\sin\theta>2m_ec^2$, with $\theta$ the angle between its direction and the field, and its
conversion rate depends on the photon energy, the field strength and that angle \cite{Erber66}; this is the process of pulsar and magnetar
magnetospheres. The arena supplies, for (b), the invariant $M_{\rm pair}$, which has the zero set of $s$, and, for (a), the canonical fields of
\cref{e7:prop:canon}. It supplies no threshold, cross section or rate, since it contains neither $m_e$ nor $e$. A pure magnetic background
creates no pairs from the vacuum, and the pairs of magnetised environments are photon-assisted; neither fact concerns the rest energy $E_0$ of
the field.

\emph{The analogy with the tower.} A magnetic field acts on momenta as a rotation, which is compact; an electric field acts as a boost, which is
non-compact (\cref{e7:prop:canon}). In the tower the criterion is commutation with $K_n$, not compactness: elements that commute with $K_n$
conserve the Fock number, and elements that anticommute with it create quanta in pairs from the Fock vacuum (\cref{e2:lem:N}(iii),(iv),
\cref{e2:prop:bogo}). The two criteria differ: the compact charge $J=e_2e_3$ creates a pair from the Fock vacuum, while the pole operators
$\omega_i$ are boosts that commute with $N$. The analogy is interpretive and rests on the commutator criterion. It does not identify the two
questions. The Schwinger generator acts on the four-momentum of a charged particle in spacetime; the generators of
\cref{e2:lem:N} act on the interior Fock space, whose number $N$ is not the number of electrons. The pairs of \cref{e2:prop:bogo} appear at first
order in the rapidity, with no threshold and no exponent, as in a change of reference vacuum, whereas the Schwinger rate is non-perturbative,
with an exponent fixed by $e$ and $m_e$, neither of which the arena contains.

\subsection{Several particles in three times}
\label{e7:sec:sixmomenta}

The six-momentum $P=\sum_i(\mathsf E_i/c)\,e_i+\sum_ap_a\,e_{a+3}$ has three temporal components (\cref{en:temporal}). Set $c=1$.

\begin{proposition}[Two six-momenta at mutual rest]
\label{e7:prop:sixmass}
Let $u_1,u_2\in V^+$ be unit vectors with $\eta(u_1,u_2)=\cos\vartheta$ and $P=m_1u_1+m_2u_2$. Then
\[
M^2:=\eta(P,P)=m_1^2+m_2^2+2m_1m_2\cos\vartheta ,
\]
so $M=m_1+m_2$ at $\vartheta=0$, $M=(m_1^2+m_2^2)^{1/2}$ at $\vartheta=\pi/2$ and $M=|m_1-m_2|$ at $\vartheta=\pi$. In $\Cl(3,3)$, $P^2=M^2\id$.
The graded rest Hamiltonian $H_g=m_1E_{(1,1)}+m_2E_{(2,1)}$ of the tower is the case of two orthogonal unit vectors of $\R^{6,6}$, $H_g^2=(m_1^2+m_2^2)\id$;
the ungraded $H_u=m_1\,e_1\otimes\id+m_2\,\id\otimes e_1$ has spectrum $\{\pm m_1\pm m_2\}$ and is not a vector.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expand $\eta(P,P)$; $V^+$ is positive definite. Vectors of a Clifford algebra square to their norm. The rest is \cref{e1:prop:rest}(i),(iii).
\end{proof}

Fixed (e7-15: symbolic; $P^2$ exactly at $\cos\vartheta=\tfrac35$; the tower exactly at $n=2$). The reading is this: the graded composite of
\cref{e1:prop:rest}, with masses in quadrature (\cref{d6:prop:dirac}(iv)), is ``two masses with orthogonal clocks'', and the additive
ungraded pair is ``two masses with one clock''. Its precise sense is limited. In $\R^{6,6}$ the temporal triples of the two copies are orthogonal,
so $\vartheta=\pi/2$ is forced between vectors of different copies; inside one copy $\vartheta$ is free. The ungraded operator agrees with the
case $\vartheta=0$ only in its largest eigenvalue $m_1+m_2$. Its additivity is that of the eigenvalues of commuting operators, one mass shell per
particle, not that of a vector sum, which has two eigenvalues, not four. The identification is interpretive.

\begin{proposition}[Uphill decays and one clock]
\label{e7:prop:uphill}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item In signature $(1,3)$, future timelike $P_1,P_2$ satisfy $\eta(P_1,P_2)\ge m_1m_2$, so $\eta(P_1+P_2,P_1+P_2)\ge(m_1+m_2)^2$: a particle
decays only into lighter ones in total.
\item In signature $(3,3)$ this fails. $P=(10,0,0;0,0,0)$ splits as $P_1=(5,5,0;1,0,0)$ plus $P_2=(5,-5,0;-1,0,0)$, with masses
$10\to7+7=14$ and $\eta(P_1,P_2)=1<49$, every clock component $\mathsf E_1$ positive.
\item The clock $e_1$ reads $m_4^2=\mathsf E_1^2-|\bp|^2=24$ for each product, and $2\sqrt{24}<10$: read by the clock the decay is downhill.
\item If all temporal parts are parallel to one unit $u\in V^+$, the momenta lie in $\R u\oplus V^-$, of signature $(1,3)$, and (i) holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\eta(P_1,P_2)=E_1E_2-\bp_1\cdot\bp_2\ge E_1E_2-|\bp_1||\bp_2|\ge m_1m_2$, the last step since
$(E_1E_2-|\bp_1||\bp_2|)^2-m_1^2m_2^2=(E_1|\bp_2|-E_2|\bp_1|)^2\ge0$ with $E_1E_2\ge|\bp_1||\bp_2|$. (ii)--(iii) Integer arithmetic. (iv) The Gram
matrix of $(u,e_4,e_5,e_6)$ is $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$.
\end{proof}

(i) is inherited; (ii) is the observation that with more than one time a particle can decay into heavier ones \cite{Dorling70}, recalled in
\cite{Tegmark97}, whose main argument is the loss of well-posed prediction; an ultrahyperbolic Cauchy problem can be made well posed by a nonlocal
constraint \cite{CraigWeinstein09}, and \cref{ex:tachyons} shows that compact surplus times give tachyons. All are checked exactly or,
for (iv), with no violation among $20000$ random pairs, against $12639$ violations without a common clock (e7-16, e7-17). Item~(iii) locates the
paradox. ``Uphill'' is uphill in the six-dimensional mass $M_6$ and downhill in the mass $m_4$ read by a clock, since $m_4(\hat n)\le M_6$ with equality iff
the temporal momentum is parallel to the clock (\cref{r2:prop:rotate}; \cref{en:temporal}).

\emph{What survives the clock reduction}. Under the reduction to one selected clock $\hat n$, the reduced shell $\boldsymbol{\mathsf E}\parallel\hat n$ of \cref{thm:wlgauge}, with the surplus temporal momenta removed by the constraints of \cref{sec:constraints} (\cref{r2:prop:shellII}), every momentum lies in $\R\hat n\oplus V^-$, of signature $(1,3)$. \begin{proposition}[Duality on a null field is a rotation about its axis]
\label{e7:prop:dualrot}
Let $z=\bB+i\bE\neq0$ be null and $\hat{\mathbf k}=\bB\times\bE/|\bB\times\bE|$, which equals $\bE\times\Btext/|\bE\times\Btext|$, the direction of
propagation. Then $\hat{\mathbf k}\times z=-iz$, $\hat{\mathbf k}\cdot z=0$, and the duality rotation $e^{\vartheta G}$, $z\mapsto e^{i\vartheta}z$, acts as the
rotation $R_{\hat{\mathbf k}}(-\vartheta)$ on $\bB$ and on $\bE$ separately. The plane wave $z=w\,f(\hat{\mathbf k}\cdot\mathbf x-t)$ with $\hat{\mathbf k}\times w=-iw$
solves $\partial_tz=-i\curl z$ (\cref{prop:RS}). For $q\neq0$ and $\vartheta\notin\pi\Z$ no rotation acts as $e^{\vartheta G}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\hat{\mathbf k}\times z=-iz$ is \cref{en:stab}(ii), from $\bB\cdot\bE=0$ and $|\bB|=|\bE|$; dotting with $\hat{\mathbf k}$ gives $\hat{\mathbf k}\cdot z=0$. Then
$e^{i\vartheta}z=\cos\vartheta\,z+\sin\vartheta\,iz=\cos\vartheta\,z-\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times z=R_{\hat{\mathbf k}}(-\vartheta)z$ by Rodrigues' formula,
and $R_{\hat{\mathbf k}}(-\vartheta)$ is real. For the wave, $\curl z=\hat{\mathbf k}\times w\,f'=-iw\,f'$ and $\partial_tz=-w\,f'$. Rotations fix $q$, while
$e^{\vartheta G}$ sends $q$ to $e^{2i\vartheta}q$.
\end{proof}

Fixed; checked on $50$ null fields at three angles to $2\times10^{-15}$, the wave equation exactly (e7-20). It is the identity of helicity and
duality on null fields \cite{Calkin65,PenroseRindler84}, \cref{ex:Gcharge}, pulled back to the frame. Physically, a duality angle
$\vartheta$ turns the plane of linear polarisation of a freely propagating wave about its line of sight by $\vartheta$, the same for all
frequencies, and only multiplies a circularly polarised wave by a phase.

\emph{A coupling model and its bound}. The arena does not say that its interior flow acts on free light. Suppose, as a model, that
it does: radiation in flight is carried by the flow $X(\tau)=e^{\mp\kappa G\tau}X_0$ (\cref{sec:resttworot}(ii)), with $\tau$ the cosmic
time and $\kappa$ constant. By \cref{e7:prop:dualrot} linearly polarised light then arrives with its plane turned about the line of sight by

need. For the CMB the propagation time is that since last scattering, $t_0-t_{\rm LSS}\approx t_0=13.79$~Gyr \cite{Planck18VI}. If the rate varied,

time-dependent duality rotation is moreover not a symmetry of the free Maxwell equations, so the coupling hypothesis needs a stated propagation

\subsection{Time-odd invariants, clocks and the constancy of masses}
\label{e7:sec:timeodd}

\emph{Three manifolds and three generations.} For three manifolds with rates $\kappa_k$ and couplings $B_{kl}$ the plaquette is
$H_{12}H_{23}H_{31}=i\plaq$ with $\plaq=B_{12}B_{23}B_{31}$, $\det[H,K]=2i\plaq(\kappa_1-\kappa_2)(\kappa_1-\kappa_3)(\kappa_2-\kappa_3)$, and interior time
reversal sends $\plaq$ to $-\plaq$, which no copy reflection undoes at $n=3$ (\cref{d7:thm:three}(ii),(iv); restated, e7-23). The Jarlskog invariant of
quark mixing has the same shape: a rephasing-invariant plaquette of the mixing matrix, nonzero only for three generations, with
$\det[M_uM_u^\dagger,M_dM_d^\dagger]$ equal to $2iJ$ times the two Vandermonde products of squared masses \cite{Jarlskog85,KobayashiMaskawa73}. The
parallel is a reading. It says that in both cases a time-odd invariant of a Hermitian $3\times3$ structure needs three labels, and that the
commutator determinant factorises through it. It does not establish CP violation in particle physics: within the three-copy interior model
the invariant $\plaq$ is a property of the interior $b$-field between manifolds, with a free magnitude, not identified with the physical CKM
invariant and not connected to a baryon-number-violating, out-of-equilibrium process (the matter and charge constructions of
\cref{ch:ch06_chiral,ch:ch07_vacuum} do not supply such a process either). Sakharov's conditions for a baryon asymmetry,
baryon-number violation, C and CP violation and departure from equilibrium \cite{Sakharov67}, are not touched. At most $\plaq$ is a candidate source
of time-odd effects, and only under a reading that identifies interior time reversal with physical time reversal.

\emph{A temporal $b$-field on the clocks of matter.} A temporal rotation turns the temporal momentum at fixed six-dimensional mass, and the mass
read by a fixed clock oscillates (\cref{e6:prop:clock}). At rest, for a libration $\phi=\theta_0\sin\omega_{\rm lib} t$ in the plane $(e_1,e_2)$ read by
$e_1$, $m_4=M_6|\cos\phi|$, which for $\theta_0<\pi/2$ is $M_6\cos\phi$, and $m_4/M_6=1-\theta_0^2/4+(\theta_0^2/4)\cos2\omega_{\rm lib} t+O(\theta_0^4)$. For a uniform
rotation at rate $\nu$ about an axis at angle $\alpha$ from the clock, with the temporal energy initially on the clock, the energy read by the clock is
$E(\hat n)/M_6=1-\sin^2\alpha\,(1-\cos\nu t)$ (fixed, e7-24), and the projected mass is its modulus, $m_4/M_6=|1-\sin^2\alpha\,(1-\cos\nu t)|$, since
$m_4^2=E(\hat n)^2$ at rest. The signed expression is the clock energy, not a mass: it becomes negative near $\nu t=\pi$ when $\sin^2\alpha>\tfrac12$, and the
modulus may be dropped exactly when $\sin^2\alpha\le\tfrac12$, which includes the small-angle regime of the bounds below (z1-6a). In that
regime both are $m_4/M_6=1-A+A\cos\omega t$. A rotation common to all matter cancels in dimensionless mass ratios, so the bounds concern differential
oscillations; all are conditional on the reading (e7-25).

\emph{Assumed couplings}. The bounds below translate data into the parameters of the reading only under couplings that the arena
does not derive. Atomic and molecular spectra are those of ordinary physics, with the masses $m_4$ read by a clock common to the apparatus, and

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Quasar absorption.} Methanol lines at $z=0.89$ give $|\Delta\mu_{pe}/\mu_{pe}|\lesssim4\times10^{-7}$ ($2\sigma$) for

\item \emph{Atomic clocks.} Yb$^+$ against Cs gives $\dot\mu_{pe}/\mu_{pe}=(-0.5\pm1.6)\times10^{-16}\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$ \cite{Huntemann14}, consistent with

\item \emph{Phases.} A bound on a drift constrains $A\omega$ only under an assumption on the phase: for $R=1-A+A\cos(\omega t+\varphi)$ the slope vanishes

frequency-dependent analysis of a time series, as in the searches for oscillating constants with atomic clocks \cite{VanTilburg15}.
\item \emph{Equivalence principle.} Add the hypothesis that inertia is $m_4$ read by the clock while gravity couples to the undivided $M_6$. Then

MICROSCOPE measured $\eta_{\rm E}({\rm Ti},{\rm Pt})=[-1.5\pm2.3({\rm stat})\pm1.5({\rm syst})]\times10^{-15}$ \cite{Touboul22}, so $|\eta_{\rm E}|<7.0\times10^{-15}$
at $2\sigma$, $|\vartheta_A^2-\vartheta_B^2|<1.4\times10^{-14}$, and $\vartheta<1.2\times10^{-7}$~rad if one body is aligned. If inertia and gravity
both couple to $m_4$, the test says nothing.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Answer.} Classical or astronomical content belongs to the inherited parts: the rest energy of a field point and its stress, the
pointwise rest fraction $\tfrac23$ of a Gaussian ensemble of fields, pair kinematics and the gamma-ray horizon, the canonical fields and the
Schwinger rate, the kinematics of several times, and the identity of duality and helicity on light. There the arena agrees with ordinary physics
and adds nothing measurable. Excluded as readings are field rest energy as dark matter and the materialisation of rest energy into pairs;
bounded within an explicit coupling model, on its unwrapped small-angle branch, is the interior flow acting on free light at the rate
$m_ec^2/\hbar$; unrestricted off-clock kinematics permits problematic channels and motivates the imposed common-clock reduction, but no
proton-lifetime exclusion or uniqueness of that cure is derived. The numerical angles and rates are sensitivity estimates or bounds on those models, not measurements of quantities of
the arena. Unconstrained, or open, are $\ell$ and with it
$\kappa$, the origin of a cosmic birefringence angle, the magnitude and meaning of the three-manifold invariant, and the readings that identify
the interior with particles. Two structures are missing before the arena can make an astronomical or cosmological prediction. One is a fixed
length $\ell$, equivalently a mass through $mc^2=\hbar\kappa$. The other is a coupling of the interior to spacetime: on which fields the interior
flow acts, how the temporal part of the six-momentum couples to clocks and to gravity, and which clock ordinary matter shares. With them, and with
the dynamics and the states or boundary conditions that fix amplitudes, phases and clock orientations, the constraints above could become tests: the angles of \cref{e7:sec:sixmomenta,e7:sec:timeodd} and the rate of \cref{e7:sec:rate} would be predicted
rather than bounded.
