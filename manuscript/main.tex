\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We investigate the real split-signature Clifford algebra $\Cl(3,3)\cong M_8(\R)$ and its graded extensions $\Cl(3n,3n)$ as a common setting
for spacetime, electromagnetic and matter constructions. The aim is to distinguish consequences of specified algebraic data from additional
assumptions, physical interpretations and compatibility obstructions. For a chosen Lorentz embedding and stated commutation requirements,
the complex structure is fixed up to sign, and the associated real Dirac formulation is equivalent to ordinary complex Dirac theory. We
construct distinct displacement and field-strength representations on $\R^{3,3}$, with no nonzero Lorentz-equivariant linear intertwiner
between the specified modules. We analyse factorised interior dynamics and a Lie--Poisson closure within a restricted ansatz, including
bounded motions in a neutral-signature model whose Hamiltonian is unbounded below. In $\Cl(9,9)$, a chosen rank-$64$ projector realises one
chiral Pati--Salam family with a right-handed neutrino; its Lorentz-invariant mass kernels form a $272$-dimensional real space.
Vacuum-selection results are established under explicit assumptions for specified potentials and sectors, while global claims for the full
radiative model remain conjectural. Electric-charge identification depends on selected vacua, and the normal-plane charge is anomalous on
the chosen matter block. Curvature identities recover the Einstein tensor of a prescribed four-metric. We also analyse obstructions to
causal selection within an invariant adjoint-scalar ansatz and formulate surplus-time constraints at the test-worldline level. The
resulting constructions identify mathematical relationships and limits of compatibility. A common action, a general interacting causal
reduction and empirical predictions remain open.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} split-signature Clifford algebras; Lorentz representations; electromagnetic duality; chiral projectors;
Pati--Salam; vacuum selection; causal reduction; computer-assisted algebra.
\end{abstract}
